Where the baseline comes from

Opening, facing-a-raise, open-vs-3-bet, SB-vs-BB and SB-limp charts start from the PokerCoaching.com full preflop charts (100bb), read cell by cell; every combo count matches the PDF legend. The PDF has no limped-pot, multiway or facing-a-4-bet charts, so the "Limpers", "Open + callers" and "I 3-bet, got 4-bet" charts are live-1/3 defaults written for this trainer (the multiway ones are built from your facing-a-raise charts).

No-ante adjustment (default). The PDF assumes an ante. Your games have none, so the blinds are the only dead money ($4) and live opens are bigger. Starting from the PDF, the trainer trims the weakest hands in each group (ranked by a Chen-formula hand score that also credits pairs and suited Aces; bluffs keep suited and Ace-high hands longest): opens 5% UTG up to 15% BTN; SB bluff-raises 40% and limps 20%; 3-bet bluffs 40%; flat calls 15% (40% from the big blind, which no longer gets the ante discount); 4-bet bluffs 40%; calls versus a 3-bet 10%. Switch to "With ante" in Practice settings to drill the original charts.

Live 1/3 reads (default on). On top of your charts, the trainer adjusts for bet size and for how live players actually play. When you open and get 3-bet, the answer depends on the size. Up to $35 is a small 3-bet: play the chart, but 4-bet bluffs fold because live players don't fold to them. $40–45 is standard (usually TT+, AQ+, AJs, KQs): 4-bet AA, KK, QQ and AK; call JJ, TT, AQs, AJs and KQs, adding 99, 88, AQo, KJs, QJs, JTs and ATs in position; fold the rest. $50 and up is big (almost always QQ+ or AK): 4-bet AA, KK and AK; call QQ–TT and AQs, adding AJs, KQs and AQo in position; fold the rest. Against $20–25 opens, bluff 3-bets become folds (strong broadway hands call instead) and speculative calls fold; pairs, suited Aces and broadways still call. Hands you've painted yourself are never changed by these reads. Turn them off in Practice settings to drill the pure charts.

Sizing the trainer grades (no ante)

Open $12–15. Isolate limpers to $15 + $5 per limper ($5 more out of position). 3-bet 3× the open in position, 4× out of position. Squeeze the same, plus 1× the open for each caller. 4-bet 2.25× the 3-bet in position, 2.5× out of position. After a 4-bet, a 5-bet is all-in. From the SB after the BB raises your limp, 3-bet to 4×. 3-bet, squeeze and 4-bet sizes round to the nearest $5.

How this works

A drill that makes your live $1/$3 play automatic. You see a spot, make one decision, and get told right or wrong in a few lines.

This is exploitative poker, not GTO

GTO is the solver's unexploitable strategy: balanced, with lots of bluffs. It's built for opponents who fight back perfectly.

Exploitative means playing against the players actually at your table. Live $1/$3 players:

  • call too much, so bluff less and value bet more
  • rarely fold to a 3-bet or 4-bet, so 4-bet for value only
  • bet big with big hands, so fold marginal hands to big bets
  • play with no ante, so ranges are tighter than the solver charts

The charts start from standard solver-style ranges, then are adjusted this way. They're yours to change: whatever you paint is what gets graded.

Preflop spots (Play tab)

Unopened pot
Folded to you. Open to $12–15, or fold.
Limpers
Raise over weak limpers: $15 + $5 per limper.
Facing a raise
3-bet, call or fold. 3× in position, 4× out of position.
Open + callers
Squeeze, call or fold. Callers rarely fold, so squeeze for value.
I opened, got 3-bet
4-bet, call or fold. Depends on the 3-bet size: $35 or less small, $40–45 standard, $50+ big.
I 3-bet, got 4-bet
Jam or fold. Live 4-bets are almost always KK+ or AK.
SB limp, BB raises
Re-raise your big hands, call or fold the rest.

Terms

GTO
Solver strategy: balanced and unexploitable.
Exploitative
Adjusting to how your opponents really play.
UTG, LJ, HJ, CO
Early, middle and late seats, in order. Earlier means tighter.
BTN, SB, BB
Button (best seat), small blind, big blind.
IP / OOP
Acting after / before your opponent after the flop.
Open
First raise.
Limp
Just call the big blind.
Iso-raise
Raise over a limper.
3-bet, 4-bet, 5-bet
The re-raises after an open.
Squeeze
A 3-bet after an open and a caller.
Value / bluff
Betting a hand that's ahead / a hand that wants a fold.
Fold equity
How often your bet makes them fold. Low live.
bb
Big blind. $300 is 100bb.
Effective stack
The smaller stack in the hand.
SPR
Stack-to-pot ratio on the flop. Over 15 is deep.
Suited / offsuit
Same suit / different suits.
Broadway
T, J, Q, K, A.
Dominated
Sharing a card with a better hand, like A9 vs AK.
Range
All the hands someone could have.
Out
An unseen card that improves your hand to a flush, straight, trips, two pair or top pair.
Rule of 4 and 2
Outs × 4 is your chance (%) to hit by the river from the flop. Outs × 2 with one card to come. Over 8 outs it overshoots, so subtract (outs − 8).
Stab
A bet after a check. Small is under half the pot, big is over.
Lead / donk
Betting into the preflop raiser.
Check-raise
Check, then raise his bet.
Wet / dry
Many draws on the board / few.
Static / dynamic
Nuts unlikely / likely to change on the turn.
